## Notes per intro

### 01_gradient_descent

**Looks 7:** The amber/magenta basin and blue/gold Apollonian forms have a coherent palette, and the fresh 90-second frame has attractive depth, although its grain and the crowded network images reduce clarity.
**Sound 6:** The 120 BPM D-minor progression, FM bass, bell lead, three-against-four arpeggio and dotted-eighth echoes provide a competent arrangement with a breakdown, but little harmonic surprise.
**Vision 6:** The descent and return to the basin create a readable frame around the effects, while the network/fractal dwell and familiar sequence of demonstrations weaken the dramatic arc.
**Wow 6:** The glowing orb inside the recursive geometry is the strongest image, but the collection lacks a sufficiently distinctive payoff to lift it beyond competent compo fare.

### 02_extended_thinking

**Looks 7:** The Clawd character, code towers and dancefloor army communicate clearly, but the full-size hero shows heavy chromatic fringe and grain, and the black terminal strip feels visually bolted on.
**Sound 7:** The chip arpeggio, pentatonic lead, recursion-section Shepard rise and final low hit form a deliberate, scene-aware score, albeit on a conventional F–C–Dm–Bb foundation.
**Vision 8:** The character's journey from code world through recursion to the giant pixel formation gives the effects a purpose and supplies actual jokes and an ending.
**Wow 7:** The character army and formation are memorable, but repeated movement patterns inferred from the shader and uneven graphic polish keep the overall craft below the strongest entries.

### 03_emergence

**Looks 8:** Liquid, gyroid architecture, city, accelerator and the sweeping white black-hole ring show substantial range, with strong cyan/gold lighting despite some washed-out bloom.
**Sound 7:** Breakbeat/four-on-the-floor contrasts, acid material, modulation and the closing Cmaj9 show arrangement work, but the late snare-velocity expression keeps growing after its apparent build section and the measured peak reaches 0.997, making the late balance a concern rather than proven audible distortion.
**Vision 6:** The chapter HUD organizes the material, yet the long succession of recognizable demo effects feels more like an ambitious reel than a single developed idea.
**Wow 7:** The black-hole sequence and breadth of rendering earn solid impact, while the limited conceptual connection between scenes prevents that breadth becoming a stronger whole.

### 04_latent_space

**Looks 7:** The gold Mandelbulb, thin-film gyroid, cyan/pink fibre tunnel and sunset water supply attractive variety, although several compositions let dense geometry overwhelm the frame.
**Sound 7:** The 144 BPM trance arrangement has a useful build and supersaw energy, with conventional Am–F–C–G harmony and little individual musical identity beyond that structure.
**Vision 6:** Noise becoming form and returning to noise is a workable frame, but the sampling metaphor and increasingly frequent scene cycling do not make the familiar effects feel like a developed story.
**Wow 6:** It is a capable, compliant package whose strongest materials are appealing, but neither the images nor the ending provide a standout signature moment.

### 05_heliograph

**Looks 7:** Teal/gold metal leaves, helicoids and luminous ribbons make an elegant sculptural vocabulary, but early subjects occupy little of the frame and the full-size hero exposes jagged metal edges and line aliasing.
**Sound 8:** The 7/8 grouping, eight voicings, inharmonic struck bars, overlapping bowed tones, fourteen-step bass and reed countermelody make this one of the most thoughtfully composed scores on paper.
**Vision 8:** The transformation of related instruments and sculptures, reunion and foldaway give the 112-second structure a consistent identity and a considered conclusion.
**Wow 7:** The orrery and projected knot are distinctive, but the restrained scale and image roughness limit the immediate compo impact relative to the musical and conceptual craft.

### 06_marginalia

**Looks 8:** Cream paper, ink hatching, vermilion sun and blue water create a convincing graphic identity, with integrated captions, though some reeds read as barcode-like repetition.
**Sound 8:** Extended electric-piano harmony and swung lo-fi percussion develop into a 180 BPM drum-and-bass section, with the coded ink-splat/snare links and return to half time giving the soundtrack meaningful structural work.
**Vision 8:** The sketchbook premise governs materials, typography and transitions, especially the inversion/full-bleed change around 58.7 seconds and the final erasure.
**Wow 8:** The change from quiet page to forceful ink performance is an effective payoff, held back from winner territory by repeated architectural motifs and the unverified quality of motion and mix.

### 07_little_water_bear

**Looks 8:** Moss, refracting water, the segmented character and blue Earth establish convincing changes of scale, while plastic-looking body surfaces and angular folded forms reveal the construction.
**Sound 8:** The walking clarinet material, harp transitions and lower, slower D-flat orbital section give the score character and connect its orchestration to the story rather than merely changing the drum pattern.
**Vision 9:** Drought, folding into a tun, orbital isolation and rain-driven revival form the clearest causal and emotional narrative in the set, with a genuine reason for each scene.
**Wow 8:** The move from tiny moss creature to Earth orbit is memorable, but the simple leg-motion implementation and occasional toy-like surfaces limit the finish of that ambitious story.

### 08_selvedge

**Looks 8:** Red/ivory weave, seams, transmitted light and frayed edges remain convincing in the full-size canopy image, with careful negative space and a consistent material language.
**Sound 7:** Formant synthesis, the three-beat shuttle, friction bridge and canopy descant support the textile idea, but the repeated modal motif and retriggering appear less developed than the visual transformations.
**Vision 8:** Thread, loom, cloth, torn strips, sails, canopy and returning bobbin form a coherent progression whose later scale changes grow directly from the opening material.
**Wow 8:** Turning the cloth into architectural shelter gives the restrained studio presentation a strong payoff, though its analytic folds and narrow palette limit variety and spectacle.

### 09_vesper

**Looks 7:** Brass organ architecture, flooded reflections and coloured windows produce a persuasive cathedral atmosphere, with a strong rose-window close-up but some very dim early images.
**Sound 4:** The lead phase uses `TAU*f*vib*t` with time-varying vibrato and global time, so differentiation introduces a frequency term proportional to elapsed time, undermining controlled tuning despite clean measured levels and an otherwise substantial organ arrangement.
**Vision 6:** The astrolabe and window sequence provide development, but repeated frontal organ views and small captions do not sustain a compelling arc over the whole duration.
**Wow 6:** The flooded cathedral is an effective setting, yet the limited visual escalation and source-level musical defect reduce confidence in the finished presentation; I did not hear the defect's actual severity.

### 10_geode

**Looks 4:** The purple strata have some atmosphere, but the ring of flat hexagonal prisms and central shaft look like simple props rather than convincing crystals.
**Sound 5:** A clean, bounded pad/FM-arpeggio score with an eight-note melody and a later drop supplies basic structure, but the familiar progression and repeated material offer limited development.
**Vision 4:** The central composition persists too long, and the kaleidoscopic section around 72–96 seconds does not substantially transform the idea.
**Wow 4:** The 4,731-byte implementation is economical and starts correctly, but compactness does not compensate for the weak materials and lack of a memorable climax.

### 11_pas_de_trois

**Looks 8:** The geometric costumes, expressive silhouettes, coloured shadows and reflective stage give the toy ballet unusual clarity, although grain and rigid limbs stop it looking completely polished.
**Sound 8:** Swung subdivision, walking bass with approach notes, Charleston brass, darker growls, a chromatic build and a late key lift provide a composed theatrical arc with strong coded synchronization opportunities.
**Vision 9:** Changes of cast, costume, stage geometry and musical character culminate logically in the tiered ensemble around 111.5 seconds, making the finale feel earned rather than appended.
**Wow 9:** The full-size tower of ninety dancers is the strongest ensemble payoff in this set, bringing colour, staging and musical design together at a scale I would regard as winner-level potential, subject to the playback caveat.

### 12_murmuration

**Looks 3:** The dusk marsh and reflections are attractive, but the supposed flock repeatedly forms obvious polygonal slabs even in the full-resolution hero, and oversized or split captions further damage the presentation.
**Sound 3:** The music multiplies an already angular-frequency pitch helper by another `2*pi`, shifting intended pitches by a factor of roughly 6.28, while the 48,000-sample beat unit at 48 kHz also conflicts with the described tempo and measured peak reaches 1.029.
**Vision 5:** Gathering, predator disturbance and reformation provide a recognizable arc, but the falcon's indistinct dash and the flock rendering make the events less legible than the premise needs.
**Wow 3:** The discrete volume layers expose the mechanism at precisely the moments that should sell collective flight, and the confirmed clipping adds another concrete craft problem.

### 13_sounding

**Looks 6:** Cold sea light against the amber lighthouse, mist and reflections gives the small boat a tasteful setting, though the cutout silhouettes and largely fixed composition limit depth and variety.
**Sound 5:** Cello, harp and foghorn form an appropriate palette, but the chord gate `1-step(2,b3)` activates on the first two beats rather than the intended second and third waltz beats, weakening confidence in the intended accompaniment.
**Vision 5:** Quiet navigation is a legitimate direction, yet the small fleet dots and modest changes do not establish enough progression or payoff across roughly two minutes.
**Wow 5:** The beam and atmosphere are competently integrated, but the late imagery offers little escalation beyond the initial attractive tableau.

### 14_remanence

**Looks 8:** Black ferrofluid under lime and white tubes has convincing glossy highlights and crisp spiked silhouettes, with some noisy detail visible beneath the forms in the full-size climax.
**Sound 7:** The shared rate table tightly connects the visual modulation to the wobble-bass design, and the drum-and-bass arrangement is purposeful, although parallel minor-ninth pads and recurring bass gestures limit melodic range.
**Vision 8:** Pool, suspended sphere, urchin, torus, spike sea and collapse are substantial variations of one material, with well-defined stages rather than unrelated effect substitutions.
**Wow 8:** The spiked field and floating forms create strong impact from 5,487 bytes, while palette restraint and repetition keep the result below the most expansive theatrical finales.

### 15_gather

**Looks 3:** Flat brown corridors, glaring strips and simple yellow discs dominate the sheets, while the ribbed objects look opaque and metallic rather than like convincing worked glass.
**Sound 2:** Positive-only percussion components, a kick phase tied to absolute sample position and sparse bar-rate events make the score rudimentary, and the 120.15-second audio buffer ends roughly fourteen seconds before the coded 134.4-second visual timeline without a corresponding musical ending.
**Vision 4:** Furnace, gathering, showroom and packing imply a process, but generic primitive arrangements and the duration mismatch prevent it becoming a satisfying continuous transformation.
**Wow 3:** The final release passed its startup retry, but its weak material illusion, basic score and incomplete audiovisual ending leave little compo-level payoff.

### 16_afterglow

**Looks 4:** Orange stellar surfaces and the teal remnant provide useful colour contrast, but mostly centred spheres, a washed-out nova and square-looking star points make the cosmic scale unconvincing.
**Sound 4:** Drone, static pads, slam and ping outline the events, yet limited melodic development and the variable-frequency kick multiplied by absolute sample position weaken the musical craft.
**Vision 5:** Growth, collapse, explosion and remnant form a clear life cycle, but most transitions amount to changes of scale and brightness rather than new visual ideas.
**Wow 4:** The explosion should carry the piece, and its blurred, overbright presentation does not deliver enough detail or scale to make that moment land strongly.

### 17_supercell

**Looks 4:** Prairie poles give a useful scale reference, but the storm often reads as a slender beige pillar beneath a flat horizontal slab, followed by an overly saturated three-band rainbow.
**Sound 4:** Shared lightning triggers give a concrete synchronization mechanism, but the snare lacks an alternating-beat mask, the kick has an absolute-time phase problem, and the limited harmonic material does little to develop the weather arc.
**Vision 6:** Formation, storm, lightning and clearing provide a coherent sequence with an actual resolution, even though the cloud rendering weakens the intended escalation.
**Wow 4:** Lightning supplies a recognizable hit, but washed-out cloud forms and the coarse rainbow prevent the ambitious volumetric premise from becoming convincing spectacle.

### 18_zugzwang

**Looks 7:** Recognizable Staunton silhouettes, turned stems, gold collars and warm/cool lighting give the board convincing object detail, but the full-size hero exposes ragged edges, smeared shadows and rather plastic-looking surfaces against an empty sky.
**Sound 8:** The written cello theme with held notes, harpsichord arpeggios, accelerating escapement ticks, breakbeat sections, quieter celesta promotion and major-colour ending form a substantial scene-aware score, with a measured peak of 0.851 and no NaN samples, although its actual timbral balance remains unheard.
**Vision 7:** The six-act progression from opening through time pressure, promotion and defeat is readable and musically coordinated, but repeated close views of three central pieces and abrupt tableau changes convey a staged chess fantasy more strongly than the promised unfolding tactical predicament.
**Wow 7:** The sliced capture and coronation rings are effective signature moments within a compliant 8,139-byte release, yet the modestly tilting king and familiar board framing offer less escalation than the strongest finales in the set.

### 19_first_mark

At 45 seconds the aurochs stands beside the ochre outline it has just left on the wall, and the return to the first handprint gives that transformation an ending. The cracked rock holds up close, but the animal's dot eye, smooth muzzle and button-like hooves make it look like a toy. The breath flute and stone taps suit the cave, though the stone rhythm loses accents because slots 4 and 6 are unreachable in its four-slot counter. The single animal beside its painting makes a stronger impression than the herd spread across the nearly uniform brown passage.

### 20_die_shrink

The first coarse patches of wafer colour resolve into individual dies, and at 58 seconds a copper trace fills the right side of the frame like a building overhang. The beeper gains sample rate and bit depth as the picture gains resolution, although the lead applies note vibrato to an oscillator running on total elapsed time, making its pitch excursion grow late in the piece. Writing the greetings into the memory array gives the descent a destination, and winding the resolution back down completes it. The luminous signals threading the metal have real scale, even when the repeated bond-wire flybys have little left to reveal.

### 21_bell_lap

The yellow 07 jersey stays easy to pick out from high in the rafters or low behind the wheels, though the neon sprint blows out some jersey detail. At the three lap-bell strikes the drums thin almost to silence, leaving space for the faster reed answer when the sprint gets underway. Each camera position answers a different race question, right down to the timing slit. At 72 seconds both bibs are readable above two almost coincident front tyres, and the red hairline and 0.012-second margin settle the race in a single image.

### 22_tin_canon

The rolled lid, key threaded through it and printed fish label make the tin recognizable before the title has even cleared. In the 6/8 score a reed takes up the plucked tune two bars late, with a third voice a fifth higher as the fish gather, though the same short subject carries much of the piece. The dive through one sardine's eye gives the cut from kitchen counter to open sea a precise point of connection. At 62 seconds hundreds of tiny fish reproduce even the eye and gill on the label, and the return to an empty tin stamped with a net weight of zero lands the joke.

### 23_off_air

At 33 seconds the tuning drum recedes from discs large enough to see their pins to a thin orange trace at its far end. Relay chatter and a swung metal hat belong to that hardware, though the returning FM hook changes little beyond its octave. A vertical-hold fault gives the flat test card a reason to tear open into the drum, and the final scanline brings the broadcast to a clean stop. The globe coming apart along its latitudes pays off the opening close-up because each coloured dot is still recognizable as a separate solid part.

### 24_tilt

The wound plunger, mirrored ball and four-rail ramp make a readable pinball table, although the flat bumper tops and broad white highlights give parts of it a plastic-toy finish. Metallic chimes pick out the targets, and the melody doubles its pace and climbs an octave for multiball. Cutting beneath the table to the plumb bob gives the tilt warning a physical cause. By 85 seconds the bob touching its ring is an almost white shape against brass, so the decisive contact is harder to see than several earlier, lower-stakes hits.

### 25_kindling

Tree trunks interrupt the firelight across the clearing, and a change to an overhead view makes the dancers' joined hands and seven-pointed star legible in the same frame. Six flint strikes lead into a 7/8 dance whose 3+2+2 accents govern both the drum and the dancers, with a half-speed flute and an ornamented reed giving the tune two distinct characters. The return to a cold fireplace gives the night a shape, although the succession of spark formations stretches the middle. The towering figures at 110 seconds are a striking change of scale, even as violet glare softens their limbs into the fire.

### 26_una_corda

At 82 seconds a narrow band of focus picks individual tuning pins out of the gold plate, giving the later flight along the strings a tangible point of departure. The 840-note nocturne varies the timing of melody against bass, lets the pedal carry notes through releases, and brings its theme back under the soft pedal after the minor-key climax. The bright horizon of strings is a bold departure, though several views of it resolve to much the same vanishing point. Each hammer and its sounding string share one scored note event, and the final pullback reveals the grand responsible for that entire landscape.

### 27_wavefolder

Reflections pick out both sides of the gold wheel at 121 seconds, though the striped sun, chrome sphere and magenta grid are familiar synthwave images. Two written themes change register and speed, the chorus chords stab in groups of 3+3+2, and the final whole-tone lift gives the long arrangement somewhere to go. The camera spends much of the 144 seconds on the same road, with the same sun ahead. Folding a second landscape overhead finally makes the wavefolder visible at the scale of the whole world and gives the last chorus its best image.

### 28_from_here

Red and blue enamel on perpendicular faces makes the same stack of brass plates spell YOU from one stop and ME from the other, with clean letter spacing even at the blue stop at 65 seconds. The electric piano leaves gaps for the horn in five-beat bars, and the shift from E minor to E major gives the second word a different inflection. Letting the viewer turn the sculpture after the music ends makes viewpoint part of the piece. The oblique angle already gives away both words early, which takes some surprise out of the long approach to the second reveal.

### 29_the_yellow_cup

Raindrops soften the lit windows across the street while the dark rolled rim makes one missing chip visible from across the kitchen. A ceramic ping marks the ripple in the tea, and the closing plucked phrase leaves a rest where its third note used to be. At 56 seconds the kitchen and the chip have both vanished, leaving a perfect cup under the line “My guess is no.” Restoring the flaw gives the ending its force, although the similar framings through the first half and the small subtitles make this an intimate piece for a compo screen.

### 30_spillway

Pale light across the flooded columns gives the surge hall depth, while the blank piers at six seconds and the broad turquoise streaks of whitewater make the dam itself hard to size. The doubling snare roll and half-bar break prepare the turbine entrance, and the higher lead gains a fifth at the switchyard. Following the water from reservoir to shaft to turbine makes the changes of scene easy to follow. The return to a reflected dam at dusk is satisfying, but the bright, sparsely detailed turbine blades give the conversion to power little mechanical weight.

### 31_zero_g

The cyan and orange cores are easy to distinguish, but the bloom flattens their facets and the small satellites nearly disappear into the background. The FM lead, ghost snares and octave lift give the swarm a second gear, and the rising snare subdivisions and reduced sample rate make the compression passage musically distinct. The star occupies much the same part of the frame while captions carry most of the change from thinking to inspecting images to exchanging messages. At 99 seconds the falling byte counter is legible, but the star and its orbit rings still look much as they did before the squeeze, weakening the payoff.

## Method and caveats

### Review of 19–31 on 2026-11-19

- I inspected each contact sheet and a separate hero image, then rendered an additional 1920 × 1080 development frame for each intro. The table below gives those frame times. I read the production notes and inspected the scene, post-processing and music shaders, plus loader code where it supplied the score or playback behavior.
- I judged composition and materials from the images. I inferred the movement between frames and the placement of musical cues from the animation and score code. I did not hear the soundtracks or watch continuous moving footage. Instrument balance, perceived timing and smoothness remain uncertain.
- I ran the repository's audio checker and packed-file startup test from PowerShell in Edge. The audio statistics describe development buffers. Some GPU work overlapped, so frame and startup times had no weight in the scores.
- The nine 16-second audio windows cover up to 144 seconds. I also measured Una Corda's entire 183.83-second stereo buffer and checked Wavefolder's final fraction of a second separately. Across the 13 new entries the measured peak stayed below full scale and the audio samples were finite. Short buffers leave empty windows that print an undefined RMS.
- On 28 and 29 the stock test reported “start screen still there” twice. Both loaders keep the title overlay in the DOM for reuse, and the tool only checks whether its element exists. I also clicked through each packed release and checked the overlay's computed visibility. The other 11 passed the stock check on their first attempt.
- Both 28 and 29 reached their endings in real time and restarted from their Replay buttons. At the end of 28, the left and right arrow keys took the sculpture to its two stops. The resulting frames read red YOU and blue ME.

### Review of 01–18 on 2026-11-17

- Read the rating instructions, project README and tool README, then each eligible intro's README and music shader; skimmed its scene shaders and inspected relevant post-processing or loader code where needed.
- Inspected all 18 contact sheets and a separate hero or Pouet still for every intro with one available. Additionally inspected full-size heroes for 02, 05, 07, 08, 11, 12, 14 and 18. For 01, which lacked a separate hero/Pouet still, rendered and inspected a fresh 1920×1080 frame at 90 seconds.
- For the follow-up review of 18, read its README, scene, music, post-processing and loader sources and repeated the same audio and packed-release startup checks. Its measured RMS ranges from 0.103 to 0.207 in the first eight sections, then 0.002 in the tail beginning at 128 seconds; no clipping or NaN samples were found. Existing scores and measurements for 01–17 were retained from the original review rather than rerun against potentially changed files.
- Did not listen to audio or watch moving playback. Musical arrangement, motion, pacing between sampled frames and synchronization are inferred from code and timelines; mixing quality, actual perceived timbre, animation smoothness and the emotional force of transitions remain uncertain. The source defects discussed above are observations about implementation, not claims of having heard their effects.
- Ran `node tools/audiostat.mjs <intro>` and `node tools/capture.mjs check check <intro>/intro.html` from PowerShell, sequentially. All releases reported ready and start screen gone, except Gather's first attempt; its one permitted retry passed. Startup success is a smoke test, not proof of fault-free full-duration playback.
- Audio measurements come from the development shader pipeline; boot checks exercise the packed release. I did not establish byte-for-byte audiovisual equivalence between those paths. All measured sample NaN counts were zero; an RMS printed as NaN for an empty section beyond a short buffer is not a NaN audio sample. Murmuration alone exceeded full scale, at 48–64 seconds.
- The test renderer identified an NVIDIA RTX 5090 through ANGLE/D3D11. Tests were sequential, but other GPU activity was not established, so frame timings did not affect scores.
- Judged observed execution rather than README claims or author reputation. Contact sheets favour composition and scene variety over motion, and source inspection favours explicit musical structure over timbral pleasure; those are material limitations, especially for Sound and Wow. No bonus was given merely for using fewer bytes, and no 10 was warranted by the evidence.
- Deleted generated review screenshots; changed no intro or tool. This Markdown is the only retained review output.
