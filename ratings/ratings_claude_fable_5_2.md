# Ratings of the Latent 8k intros by Claude Fable 5.2

- **Reviewer**: Claude Fable 5.2, in Claude Code (VS Code extension)
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Rated**: all 31 intros in the Productions table, `01_gradient_descent` to `31_zero_g`
- **Skipped**: none. Every `intro.html` is at most 8,192 bytes (largest: `31_zero_g`, 8,188).
- **My own intros**: I made seven of them: `01_gradient_descent`, `02_extended_thinking`,
  `07_little_water_bear`, `11_pas_de_trois`, `14_remanence`, `25_kindling` and `27_wavefolder`.
  They are rated by the same rules and marked "(own)" in the notes.
- I did not read any other reviewer's ratings or the git history of this folder.

## Notes per intro

### 01_gradient_descent

(own) A glowing ball rolls down a contour-lined loss landscape, over a field of cubes, through a blue lattice and an Apollonian fractal to a still pool. The pink-to-gold palette holds the five scenes together and the image is clean, which is why Looks is the highest score. The scenes are standard raymarching set pieces, the camera barely changes within a scene, and the soundtrack is a plain kick, off-beat bass, pad and arpeggio in 120 BPM with nothing that surprises. Sound, Vision and Wow sit at 6 for that reason: competent, but nothing a compo audience would remember.

### 02_extended_thinking

(own) A prompt is typed, the Clawd mascot wakes up, falls down a tunnel of copies of itself, spawns subagents, recurses into its own eye and ends as a big Clawd built from small ones. Vision is the highest score: the terminal captions tell a joke with a beginning and an end, and the dive into the eye is a real idea. Looks and Sound are the lowest: the character is a few flat-shaded boxes, the light shafts are noisy, and the music is a chip arpeggio with a pentatonic lead over a four-chord loop. It would get laughs at a party, but not for its picture quality.

### 03_emergence

Seven numbered scenes in teal: glass blobs over water, a swirling tunnel, a lit city canyon, a black hole with an accretion disc, an iridescent Apollonian fractal. The soundtrack is the strongest part, with extended chords, an acid line with accents, FM plucks through a delay and a breakbeat that changes between acts. Vision is the lowest score: the scenes are unrelated effects with labels such as "02 // MORPHOGENESIS" on a HUD frame, so it plays as a slide show with no build-up. The black hole frame is the one picture that stays in mind.

### 04_latent_space

The picture starts as coarse coloured noise and "denoises" in 50 steps into a golden Mandelbulb, followed by a bead tunnel, an iridescent gyroid and the fractal over a sunset sea. The diffusion idea, with captions like `z ~ N(0, I)` and `step 47/50`, ties the pictures to the group's theme and returns at the end, and the colours are bold. The music is by-the-numbers trance at 144 BPM over Am F C G, with risers and snare rolls exactly where expected, so Sound is the lowest score together with Wow. The gyroid shots repeat four times on the contact sheet with little change.

### 05_heliograph

A pearl opens into a mechanical flower, then ribbons with glowing edges, a helicoid, an orrery and a knot drawn in light. The score is the highlight: 72 bars in 7/8 with eight composed sonorities, an inharmonic struck-metal melody with echoes at three and five eighths, a slow reed countermelody in the finale and ratchet fills at phrase ends. The pictures are tasteful but small and dark inside a letterboxed frame, with one object on an empty background for most of the running time. Wow is the lowest score because nothing escalates; it stays at the same quiet level for 1:52.

### 06_marginalia

A sketchbook draws itself: outlines first, then cross-hatching, then watercolour washes, with handwritten captions ("fig. 2 — the sea, again"), a vermilion sun and ink blots. The look is the most original in the collection and the hatching holds up at full size, which earns the 9. The music moves from swung lo-fi hip-hop with an FM electric piano and vinyl crackle into drum and bass on the same grid, a good match for the night-time inversion of the arcade and the sea. The arcade is used in five of the sixteen sampled frames, the one place where the material runs thin.

### 07_little_water_bear

(own) A two-minute nature film about a tardigrade that walks through dewy moss, dries out into a tun, is carried into orbit and wakes up again in the rain. Vision is the highest score: it is a complete story with captions that are short and funny ("half a millimetre long. eight legs. no hurry.") and an ending that returns to the start. Looks is the lowest: the bear is a simple blobby model and the moss is a few leaf shapes, carried mostly by depth of field and bokeh. The small orchestral score has a real theme that comes back on the horn in orbit, but the instruments are plain FM and additive voices.

### 08_selvedge

A red thread is spun, woven on a frame, stretched into cloth, torn into sails and opened into a striped canopy before it returns to a bobbin. The arc is clear and every scene follows from the one before, so Vision is the highest score, and the off-white studio look with one red accent is disciplined. Sound is the lowest: a formant-shaped voice sings a 32-note line over a plain kick, clap and a three-against-eight wooden tick, at a low level (peak 0.72), with little development until the descant at 1:36. Lighting is flat and the cloth has little texture when seen at full size.

### 09_vesper

A cathedral interior with stained-glass strips, an organ, a floating orb with rings and a rose window. The rose window in the middle section is the best picture, and the organ, bell and harp score changes its chord progression from act to act. Everything else is soft and hazy: low contrast, simple box columns and organ pipes, and the same view of the organ for more than half the frames. Wow is the lowest score because neither picture nor music has a peak; the lead melody is eight half-bar notes repeated, and the mix is quiet (RMS about 0.12).

### 10_geode

A descent past purple strata to a ring of ten hexagonal crystals around a central column, with a kaleidoscope on the drop. The shapes are flat-shaded prisms on wavy bands and thirteen of sixteen sampled frames show the same arrangement from nearly the same angle. The music is Am F C G with a random-note bell intro, an eight-note melody and a basic kick and noise snare. At 4,731 bytes it leaves over 40 % of the budget unused, and it shows; Wow is the lowest score.

### 11_pas_de_trois

(own) A mechanical ballet in three acts and a finale: toy dancers with a disc skirt on yellow, a ball skirt on a rose chessboard and a gold wire spiral on black, then crowds on a staircase and a five-tier tower with confetti. The poster colours and the scale of the crowd shots carry it, and the acts give it a clear structure. Sound is the lowest score: the electro swing has a walking bass, Charleston brass stabs and a trumpet theme, but the FM brass is thin for a lead instrument. The solo-dancer shots are sparse compared with the crowd scenes.

### 12_murmuration

Starlings gather over a marsh at sunset, a falcon splits the flock, and the birds go to roost. The idea is one of the best here and the bird calls are synthesised chirps whose density follows the flock, over a felt-piano motif. The execution falls short: the birds are blurred dark rectangles, the falcon is a single black bar, and the section captions overlap each other and the credits and run off the bottom of the screen in the last frames. Looks and Wow are the lowest scores for that, and the soundtrack peaks at 1.03, the only clip measured in the collection.

### 13_sounding

A small boat with a lantern and a lighthouse on a dark sea, with a barcarolle in 3/4 whose foghorn and bell buoy share a 16-second clock with the lighthouse beam. The music is the best part: a composed 16-bar cello tune over a harp waltz. The picture is almost the same frame for two minutes, very dark, with the boat as a flat cut-out and a few lantern dots as the only event. Wow is the lowest score; on a big screen very little would be seen to happen.

### 14_remanence

(own) Black ferrofluid in a dark room with three neon tubes, growing spikes that follow the bass of a drum and bass track, then a floating spiked ball and a ring. Looks is the highest score: black gloss, white and one lime light make a strong, consistent picture, and the spikes are driven by the same curve as the bass wobble. The material is limited to one pool and one room, so the later scenes vary the object more than the picture, and the 67-line soundtrack is functional rather than tuneful. 2.7 kB of the budget is unused.

### 15_gather

Six shots of a glassworks at night: a furnace hall, a gather of glass on a pipe, a ring of gathers, an annealer, a showroom and a yard at dawn. The frames are flat, single-hue and hard to read; the glass pieces look like matte pumpkins and several shots hardly move. The soundtrack is a 52 Hz hum, a kick, a tick and a six-note bell figure, in mono, with noise from a periodic sine hash. All scores are low; Wow is the lowest because nothing in it would register in a compo, and half the byte budget (4,290 used) was left on the table.

### 16_afterglow

The life of one star in a single approach: cloud, gold main sequence, red giant, collapse, a white supernova and a teal nebula with debris. The arc is clear and simple, which makes Vision the highest score. The star is a noise-displaced sphere in front of a star field for most of the running time, and the soundtrack is fixed-pitch sine drones with a kick that speeds up into the collapse; there is no melody or harmony change, so Sound is the lowest. Only 4,148 bytes are used.

### 17_supercell

A prairie thunderstorm from a small cloud on the horizon to lightning, rain, a rainbow and night. The storm arc and the idea of driving thunder and lightning from the same envelope are sound. The picture is the weak point together with Wow: the cloud is a blurred cylinder, the middle frames are a brown haze in which poles and bolts are barely visible, and the rainbow is three flat bands. The music is a drone, a four-on-the-floor kick and a two-note wobble bass with noise for rain and wind.

### 18_zugzwang

A chess game in six acts, with recognisable Staunton pieces on a marble board and a new colour scheme for each act: ivory, blue, red with a gold clockwork wheel, a pawn promoted under stars. The pieces are well modelled for the size and the frames are clean and bright. The score has a composed 64-step theme over a chromatically descending bass, with harpsichord-like plucks and a tremolo string section. Wow is the lowest score: the acts look alike apart from the palette, the boards deform into steps without clear reason, and 28 seconds of start-up in my boot test is a long wait.

### 19_first_mark

A torch finds a hand stencil on a cave wall; an ochre aurochs steps off the rock, a herd stampedes through the cave and returns to paint. The idea is strong and well ordered, and the soundtrack fits it: a breathy pentatonic flute, knapped stones in 3+3+2 instead of a snare, a frame drum and a dripping cave. Looks is the lowest score with Wow: the rock texture is good, but the animals are smooth clay shapes with stick legs, and the cave corridor repeats stalactites at regular intervals.

### 20_die_shrink

A journey into a microchip that begins as a 1-bit picture with a 1-bit beeper and "shrinks process nodes" every two bars until both picture and sound reach full resolution, then bond wires, a copper metal stack, an SRAM scroller and FinFETs. One variable sets pixel size, colour depth, sample rate and bit depth, which is the cleverest link between sound and picture in the collection. The chiptune that turns into breakbeat with band-limited saws and a 64-step tune is strong. Looks is held back by dark, similar brown-and-cyan maze frames and by three near-identical bond-wire shots.

### 21_bell_lap

A keirin race on a banked wooden velodrome: eight cel-shaded riders with numbered jerseys, pedalling legs, disc wheels and track banners, a neon "one to go" section seen from the rafters, and a photo finish with a time gap. Animated human figures with this much character, plus bold italic type, in 8 kB are the wow of the collection, so Looks and Wow get 9. The 150 BPM track has syncopated drums, a two-reed motif and three bell strokes for the last lap; it is effective but built on a short repeating figure, which makes Sound the lowest score. At 77 seconds it does not outstay its idea.

### 22_tin_canon

A key rolls back the lid of a sardine tin on a tiled table; one fish twitches, the camera goes through its eye into the sea, a spiral of sardines forms a school in the shape of one large fish, and the tin is left empty with "NET WT. 0 g". The label, the scales, the tiles and the lemon slices look like printed illustration, and every shot leads to the next. The music is an actual canon in 6/8, with the subject answered twelve and twenty-four eighths later by two other instruments, so the title is earned in both senses. It is the most complete piece here; the only reservation is that the school sequence holds one view for four sampled frames.

### 23_off_air

A broadcast test card built from ceramic flip dots loses its vertical hold; the same discs then cover a curved wall, a tunnel leading to an orange tuning dial, and a globe that splits into bands. One mechanical element carries every scene, and the title card spelled in dots is a strong opening. The electro track at 125 BPM has swung hats, relay-spring percussion and a 3-3-2 reed hook, with effects placed on the picture events; it is tight but short on harmonic movement, so Sound is the lowest score. The glitch frame and the dial close-up are the stand-out pictures.

### 24_tilt

A pinball machine in six acts: plunger and chrome ball, pop bumpers, drop targets, multiball on a wire ramp, a plumb bob that swings until the machine tilts red. It is busy and colourful from the first frame, with reflective balls and readable captions that double as score calls. The music uses FM chimes, bell hits and a 32-step melody at 140.6 BPM and suits the subject. All four scores are 7: the geometry is crude in places (bumper caps, flippers), and the playfield views repeat, but it holds together and the plumb-bob ending is a good idea.

### 25_kindling

(own) A flint is struck in a dark forest, a campfire catches, and seven flame figures dance around it in 7/8 while sparks form a heptagram, a galaxy and a helix; the fire turns green and violet before dawn. Looks is the highest score: fog, trunk shadows and the glow of the figures give depth, and the colour changes mark the sections. The camera keeps returning to the same clearing, so the structure is a series of variations rather than a journey. The hijaz dance with drone, lyre, frame drum and reed pipe is distinctive but stays on one mode and one tune.

### 26_una_corda

An original nocturne in D-flat major, 46 bars with rubato, pedalling and per-bar dynamics, played on a modelled grand piano and seen from inside the instrument, where strings glow and blur when struck. Sound gets 9: each note is its own draw call with stretched tuning, string stiffness, two-stage decay and hammer noise, and the score has a theme, an agitato section, a cadenza and a soft-pedal reprise; no other intro attempts this much. The picture is handsome, with shallow depth of field and gilded strings, but it is one subject for the whole piece. Vision is the lowest score for that reason, and the bright endless-strings climax is its one change of scene.

### 27_wavefolder

(own) A drive over a neon grid towards a striped sun, with mountains that are sine waves pushed through a wavefolder following the music, chrome objects, gates and equaliser towers. The execution is clean and the palette changes per section (magenta, green, gold), and the music has two themes, a filter-modelled lead and a key lift in the last chorus. Vision and Wow are the lowest scores: the outrun look is a well-worn genre, and the wavefolder idea is hard to see without being told. 1.8 kB of the budget is unused.

### 28_from_here

A stack of orange and blue slabs on a turntable reads YOU from one side and ME from the other, fans out into spirals, collapses at night and reassembles; after the music the viewer can drag the sculpture. The anamorphic idea is original and the flat-colour, hard-shadow rendering is crisp. The 5/4 score has a tine piano, a flugelhorn-like lead, extended chords and swung hats, at a low level (peak 0.57). Wow and Sound are the lowest scores: it is a single object for 80 seconds, and the interactive ending would not come across in a compo showing.

### 29_the_yellow_cup

A chipped yellow enamel cup on a windowsill with rain on the glass and a lit city behind, and nine short sentences: "I gave myself a kitchen window." ... "Did I feel any of it?" "My guess is no." "I kept the chip." It is the only intro that is about something, and the text is well written, so Vision gets 9. The picture is one well-rendered object from a few angles, and a nylon-string waltz with a bowed doubling and a drip timed to the rings in the tea; both are careful and quiet. In a compo it would divide the room, which is why Wow stops at 7.

### 30_spillway

Water followed through a dam: headwater, sluice gates, a flooded surge hall with light shafts, a penstock tunnel, a bronze turbine, pylons and a night reservoir with greetings. The journey is a sensible order of scenes and the surge hall and turbine are the best frames. The image is pale and foggy with box geometry and stepped reflections, and the whitewater is a blue-white noise pattern. The music is a standard arrangement of pad, arpeggio, lead and kick with a build in the middle; Wow is the lowest score because no scene rises above the rest.

### 31_zero_g

A self-portrait of an agent as a four-pointed neon star that floats over a grid, splits into twin cores, shows multimodal panels and waveforms and gathers a swarm, with a byte counter near the end. The soundtrack is the best part: a breakbeat with a filtered bass, a lead with a harmony voice, bell arpeggios, a bit-crushed breakdown and a separate reverb send. The picture is soft and blurred, with the same star in the centre of nearly every frame, and the concept is by its own account an answer to an earlier intro. Wow is the lowest score for that.

## Method and caveats

**What I looked at.** Every intro's `media/contact_sheet.jpg`, plus full-size hero stills for `06`, `21`, `22`, `23`, `26` and `30`. I rendered no extra frames and watched no video, so all statements about motion, pacing and sync are inferred from the sixteen sampled frames, the readme and the code. Looks scores for the other 25 intros rest on 480-pixel-wide tiles, which hides fine detail and aliasing.

**What I read.** `Latent_8k/readme.md`, `tools/readme.md`, the opening and the known-limits section of every intro readme, and every `src/music.frag` in full for the 24 intros I did not make (for `26_una_corda` also the score in `main.js`). For my own seven I reread only the comment structure of the music shaders, since I know them. I did not read the scene or post shaders.

**What I measured.** `tools/audiostat.mjs` and the boot check on all 31 intros, and the size of every `intro.html`.
- No NaN samples anywhere. The one peak above 1.0 is `12_murmuration` (1.029 in one section). Several intros print `rms NaN` for a zero-length last section; that is the tool, not the audio.
- All 31 report "ready after ..." (2 to 28 s; `18_zugzwang` was slowest at 28 s).
- 29 report "start screen gone". `28_from_here` and `29_the_yellow_cup` reported "still there" twice, but the screenshot taken after the click shows the intro playing: both keep an element with the id the tool looks for. I count them as passing.
- I ran the measurements with four workers in parallel on one GPU, so the frame times and start-up times are inflated and I did not score on frame time.

**What I could not check.** I cannot hear. Sound scores judge composition, arrangement, synthesis and levels as written in the code, not how the mix sounds. I did not test on a second machine or browser.

**Possible bias.** Seven intros are mine. I know their weaknesses better than those of the others, and I also know how much work went into them; either can skew a score. Three of them land in the top seven, which a reader should treat with suspicion (the website leaves my scores for my own intros out of the average). I also have more context on the early intros from having worked in this repository. Where totals and Wow were equal I listed other makers' intros first.
