## Notes per intro

### 11 Pas de Trois (34/40)

The most directed piece in the folder. Three acts
and a finale, each with its own colour, costume, floor and tune (swung electro
swing, trumpet, walking bass), built on a Busby Berkeley idea of geometry: rings,
staircase of dancers, a five-tier tower with 90 dancers. Art direction is bold
and consistent, and it is the only one that is *funny and joyful*. Weak spots:
very busy late in the piece, and the finale is the most expensive stretch.

### 06 Marginalia (33/40)

The most original *look*: a sketchbook with hatching,
watercolour wash, vermilion sun and ink splatter. Non-photoreal rendering is
rare in 8k and it sticks in the memory. The lo-fi to drum'n'bass switch at the
halfway mark and the white-on-black pages give it real pacing. The end (erase,
title, seal) is well judged. The soundtrack is solid but conventional.

### 14 Remanence (32/40)

Ferrofluid in a black room with one lime accent, cut to
171 BPM drum and bass. The same wobble curve drives the bass filter and the
spike height, which is the kind of audio-visual coupling a compo audience
rewards. Tight, single-minded, and the "field switched off" ending is neat.
Held back by being one material in one palette; the opening of 03 Emergence
also used ferrofluid.

### 07 Little Water Bear (32/40)

A nature film in 20 shots: tardigrade, dew,
dried "tun", orbit, rain, revival. The best storytelling in the folder, and
depth of field, bokeh and translucent drops are lovely. The orchestral score
(clarinet theme, strings, timpani, hall reverb) is the most "composed" score
here. The creature reads slightly like clay; one or two shots are soft.

### 18 Zugzwang (32/40)

A chess game as an architectural film in six acts
(opening, gambit, zeitnot, eighth rank, zugzwang, checkmate): turned Staunton
pieces in ivory and obsidian with gold collars, marble boards that fracture
into terraces, a brass escapement wheel behind the board in the crimson act,
a pawn that climbs to the eighth rank and crowns inside two astrolabe rings,
and a Black King that tilts and falls. Each act has its own palette, the
subject is instantly readable, and the depth of field and materials are
polished. The score is a real piece: a 64-step D harmonic minor theme on
harpsichord and cello, celesta and timpani, then breakbeat, with a reverb
tail. Held back by a quiet climax (the king's fall is slow), some marble and
crown-edge artefacts, repeated board-and-pieces framing from shot to shot,
and a readme that says 8,187 bytes while the file is 8,139.

### 20 Die Shrink (33/40)

Moore's law as a form: the picture starts as a 1-bit wafer and every two bars gains resolution, colour and bit depth, with the chiptune upgrading in step, then dives into copper canyons, an SRAM scroller, a clock tree that fires on the beat and FinFETs, and shrinks back to one bit. It is the smartest picture-and-sound coupling in the folder (Vision 9). Held back because the contact sheet repeats three shot types (bond-wire arches, copper canyon, wafer) and the middle is one brown-copper palette.

### 22 Tin Canon (32/40)

A lithographed sardine tin is wound open, the fish twitch, and the camera dives through an eye into a school that spells out the label's fish. The one-outline-four-jobs trick makes it legible and charming, and the flat graphic fish with tiled floor and lemons are the most distinctive art direction of this batch. The 6/8 pluck and reed tune is simple but suits it. Short at 77 s, and it ends on a quiet gag instead of a peak.

### 25 Kindling (32/40)

A foggy forest clearing where a fire is struck, seven flame dancers circle it in 7/8, and sparks fall into a galaxy, helix and sphere before a green and violet salt burn and a blue dawn. Atmosphere, volumetric trunks and the colour changes are the strongest of the batch, and the closing drum blow is a real moment. The dancers are soft blobs and only the trunks cast shadows.

### 26 Una Corda (32/40)

A nocturne on a modelled concert grand, seen from inside the piano, with strings lighting up per note and a horizon of strings for the climax. The score is the best Sound in the folder: 840 notes, inharmonic partials, rubato and pedal, rising smoothly from RMS 0.07 to 0.15. The picture is mostly dark, shallow-focus macro and the same string-bed framing repeats, so Wow is lower than the music deserves.

### 21 Bell Lap (31/40)

Eight cel-shaded riders in a banked velodrome, a bell-lap switch to neon lane ribbons, and a slit-scan photo finish decided by 0.012 s. The riders, jerseys and heavy italic typography look like finished motion graphics, and the finish photo is a good last shot. The music is a competent 150 BPM breakbeat with reeds; the race is short and the middle shots are similar.

### 28 From Here (30/40)

A stack of 21 plates reads as YOU from one side and ME from the other, rotates, explodes and re-forms, then lets you drag the turntable after the music ends. The single idea is excellent and executed exactly with orthographic stops, which earns the top Vision. The picture is a pastel toy with little lighting, and the five-beat piano and horn tune is thin.

### 23 Off Air (29/40)

A wall of ceramic flip-dot discs becomes a test card, loses vertical hold, rolls through a tuning drum and ends as a globe that comes apart. The disc material and the glitch tear are strong, and the globe-to-black-card finish is neat. Colours are busy (blue, orange, olive, cream everywhere) and the pace is the same slow move for most of the run.

### 24 Tilt (29/40)

An arcade pinball in six acts, from the plunger spring to jackpot multiball, the brass plumb-bob tilt switch and a red TILT ending. Bright colour and chrome balls give the most fun of the batch, and the tilt twist is a good joke. Bumper shapes are odd, large areas are flat blue, and the ball path is choreographed rather than simulated. The funk score is solid but the last 16 s are almost silent.

### 19 First Mark (28/40)

A torch finds a hand stencil, pigment becomes an aurochs that walks off the wall and leads a herd through a cave before the paintings are buried. Strong narrative and an unusual subject. The animals look like orange clay toys, the whole picture stays in one brown palette, and the sound is quiet in the middle (RMS 0.048 at 32 s).

### 29 The Yellow Cup (28/40)

A chipped cup on a rainy window, with a short first-person text about memory and whether the maker felt anything. The cup, wet glass and window city are well made, and the text gives it a voice. It is the quietest piece of the lot: the camera barely moves, there is no build, and it would not stand out against flashier compo entries.

### 27 Wavefolder (27/40)

A synthwave drive over wireframe terrain whose mountains are folded sine waves, with a striped sun, chrome sphere, wheel stations and a palette change on the last chorus. Clean and consistent, and the wave-folding idea is carried through picture and music. Synthwave is the most common 8k look and the camera never leaves the road, so it reads as familiar.

### 31 Zero-G (27/40)

A faceted Gemini star splits into twin cores, reads contact sheets, runs a swarm and watches a live byte counter crush it to 8,192, ending on a summary tablet. The self-referential gag and counter are funny, which is where the Vision score comes from. The picture is soft and blurry with few real materials, and the mix dips at 48 s (RMS 0.08).

### 30 Spillway (26/40)

A dam, sluice, surge hall, penstock, turbine and insulator avenue in six acts, on a 128 BPM melodic electronic track. The turbine and the oculus-lit hall are the best frames and the music is full and loud. Most frames are pale, washed out and hazy, and the opening acts have blown-out whites and blocky walls.

### 04 Latent Space (31/40)

Opens as noise that denoises step by step into a
Mandelbulb, ends by noising it again, and the on-screen step counter makes
the concept visible. Saturated and iridescent. Four visual families on a
144 BPM drop structure. Strong opening and bridge; the middle is a bit busy.

### 08 Selvedge (29/40)

A red thread becomes weave, cloth, sails, canopy, and
returns to a bobbin. Beautiful cream-and-red palette, a convincing cloth with
fringe and stitching, a clean arc. The score is the weak spot: very quiet in
places (RMS 0.03 in the first 16 s) and rhythmically thin.

### 05 Heliograph (29/40)

The most ambitious *music*: 7/8, grouped 3+2+2,
reed countermelody, struck-metal and glass voices, no reuse of earlier
material. The visuals are refined (metal flower, ribbons, armillary sphere,
light knot) but dark and restrained. Lacks a peak moment. Packed to 48 bytes
under the limit.

### 03 Emergence (28/40)

The biggest effect list: ferrofluid, gyroid, Menger
city, toroid tunnel, black hole, Apollonian, eclipse. The black hole and the
Menger canyon are the best frames. It reads as a tour of the usual 4k effects
rather than a single idea, and the 303 acid / breakbeat music is competent
and loud. 18 bytes to spare.

### 02 Extended Thinking (28/40)

Clawd and Claude Code jokes: prompt typed,
thinking, subagent army, recursion into the eye, compaction. Clear story and
humour, and the eye recursion is a nice trick. Visually simple boxy geometry,
and the meme depends on the audience knowing the mascot.

### 01 Gradient Descent (26/40)

Loss landscape, weights, network, fractal,
minimum. Good lines and glow, tidy concept, contour lines spaced to the beat.
The mix is dense (RMS around 0.38 throughout). The earliest and least risky of
the set.

### 12 Murmuration (24/40)

A lovely subject (a starling flock, a falcon, a
roost) and a clear five-part narrative. But the flock reads as dark blocks in
soft haze, the contrast is low, the audio peaks at 1.03 (a clip) around 48 s,
and the credit and chapter text overlaps and clips in the final frames.

### 17 Supercell (24/40)

Ambitious volumetric storm, with thunder generated
from the same law as the lightning so strike and boom are one. The music is
well structured. The picture is murky and blurry in most of the frames I saw,
and the author never saw it (see caveats).

### 09 Vesper (23/40)

A cathedral with pipe organ, rose window and astrolabe.
The rose window is the high point. Elsewhere the image is dark and murky, and
the audio is very quiet for the first 32 s (peak 0.15 to 0.24). The pacing is
even throughout.

### 13 Sounding (23/40)

A lighthouse and a lantern boat at night. The audio
design is smart (a barcarolle in 3/4 whose bar count matches the 16 s beam
revolution). But the camera barely moves, the boat is a flat cut-out, and the
contact sheet frames are almost identical. Pleasant mood, no development.

### 16 Afterglow (22/40)

The life and death of a star as one continuous
approach; the timing of collapse, held dark and flash is the best idea in the
bottom half. The star is a noisy blob, the supernova washes out to white, and
the soundtrack is simple additive sines. Not eyeballed by its author.

### 10 Geode (19/40)

Purple strata and hexagonal crystals; flat shading,
little variation between shots, simple A-minor pad and arpeggio. Fits in
4.7 kB but does not use the budget for anything memorable.

### 15 Gather (16/40)

A glassworks in six shots. Flat-lit, muddy shapes that
are hard to read as glass, long static shots, and a sparse hum / kick / bell
track. The smallest ambition per byte of any intro in the folder.

## Patterns

- **The top six all pick one strong, specific idea and carry it through the
  picture, the music and the structure.** The bottom five are mostly moods
  (night sea, cathedral, star) without something happening.
- **Small size is not the problem.** 10, 15 and 16 are the three smallest and
  the three weakest, but 01 (6.1 kB) beats them and 07 and 11 (about 7.5 to 8 kB) beat them
  by far, and 15 and 16 had room left. Ambition was the limit, not budget.
- **Sound is the biggest spread.** 11, 14, 05 and 07 read as composed music
  (themes, chord progressions, arrangement) rather than layers generated by
  rule. 09, 10, 12, 15 and 16 are the quietest or thinnest.
- **The bottom group (09, 10, 13, 15, 16) are mood pieces:** one atmospheric
  subject, a slow build, no real drop or turn. 17 has a drop but its picture
  does not carry it.

## How this was done, and what it can't tell you

- **Visuals** were judged from each intro's contact sheet (16 frames) and its
  readme timeline. I did not watch any intro play, so motion quality, camera
  work and sync to the music are inferred from the shaders and the readmes, not
  seen.
- **Sound** was judged from the `music.frag` source, the readme description
  and `tools/audiostat.mjs` (peak, RMS and NaN count per 16 s). Nobody has
  heard these tracks, me included. Audiostat found no NaNs in any intro, and
  one clip (12 Murmuration, peak 1.029).
- **Boot test:** `capture.mjs check` loaded every `intro.html` from `file://`,
  reached "click to start", and cleared the title screen on click. All 18
  pass (14 and 15 failed once with "start screen still there" on a loaded GPU
  and passed on rerun). All 18 are at or under 8,192 bytes (18 Zugzwang is 8,139; its readme still
  says 8,187).
- **Frame times are not reliable here.** My audiostat run shared the GPU with
  another agent working on 18, so later intros show much higher numbers than
  their readmes claim (for example 15 Gather: about 15 ms measured against
  0.5 ms claimed). I did not use frame time to score. Self-reported costs:
  14 Remanence reaches 7.8 ms at its climax, 11 Pas de Trois and 06 Marginalia
  are the heaviest of the top six.
- **18 Zugzwang** was rated last, on an idle GPU: about 1 ms per frame at
  1080p (matching its readme), scene shader compile about 5 s, audio peaks 0.52
  to 0.85 with no NaNs. Like the others, I judged it from the contact sheet, the
  hero still, the readme and the music source, not from playback.
- **Calibration** is from my general knowledge of 4k/8k compo winners, not from
  fresh research or a side-by-side with real party entries.
- 16 Afterglow and 17 Supercell were built while their authors' vision review
  was down, so those looks were verified numerically, not by eye. The ratings
  above are from the contact sheets.
- Scores are one reviewer's judgement; a second reviewer would likely move
  individual scores by a point or two and might swap the order of places 3 to 6.

## Second session (intros 19 to 31, 2026-11-19)

- **What I did:** read the rating instructions and my own earlier file only; looked
  at the 16-frame contact sheet of each intro; read each readme's timeline, concept and
  known limits (not the shader code); ran `audiostat` and the boot test on all 13.
  Nothing was watched or heard, so motion, camera work and sync are inferred from
  the readmes and contact sheets, as in the first session.
- **Boot test:** all 13 reach the ready screen. 19 to 27, 30 and 31 report "start
  screen gone". **28 and 29 report "start screen still there" on two runs each.** Their
  `main.js` hides the overlay with `style.display='none'` rather than removing it
  (29, line 66), and the tool tests whether the element exists, so I count this as a
  tool false negative, not a bug (for 28 this is inference; I did not read its code
  or watch it play).
- **Size:** all 13 are under 8,192 bytes (largest: 31 at 8,188, 24 at 8,179, 26 at 8,178).
- **Audio:** no NaNs and no peak above 1.0 in any of the 13 (20 Die Shrink peaks
  at 0.995, close to clipping). Silent later sections in 21, 22, 23, 28 and 29 are
  just the intros being shorter than the 144 s grid. 19 First Mark is quiet at 32 s
  (RMS 0.048).
- **Frame time:** not used for scoring. Readmes claim 0.5 to 11.7 ms; my run showed
  20 Die Shrink at 9.7 ms at t=40, the heaviest.
- **Calibration:** I tried to keep the scale of the first 18, so totals are
  comparable. A second reviewer might move 22, 25, 26 and 21 by a point either way.
  The Sound 9 for 26 Una Corda rests on the readme's description of the piano model
  and the level curve; the mix itself is unheard.
